How Plicbox works

Which accounts work, what syncs, and what's under the hood

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.
  • Microsoft Edge WebView2, which shows email and the editor. It comes with Windows 11 and with current Windows 10.
  • An email account that offers IMAP, or a Google or Microsoft account. No Plicbox account is needed.

Mail providers

Plicbox reads and sends mail over IMAP and SMTP, the standard ways mail apps connect. Almost every provider offers them; some want a setting switched on or a special password first, and Plicbox tells you which when you add the account.

Adding an account

Type your address. Plicbox works out the rest, in this order, and stops at the first answer:

  1. Known providers: Gmail, iCloud, Yahoo, Outlook.com and others are built in.
  2. Who runs the domain's mail: the domain's MX records show when your own domain is hosted by Google, Microsoft, iCloud+, Zoho (in its region) or Fastmail.
  3. What the domain says itself: the server records some domains publish (SRV), or a settings file on the domain's own server.
  4. Mozilla's settings database: the public list Thunderbird uses, which knows thousands of providers and hosts. Only the part of your address after the @ is looked up.
  5. Trying the usual names: imap.yourdomain, mail.yourdomain and similar.

If none of that works, you can type the server names and ports yourself. When something fails, Plicbox says why in plain words: a wrong password, a provider that wants an app password, or a shared host whose security certificate carries the server's own name (Plicbox then suggests that name).

Google and Microsoft accounts sign in on their own pages in your browser. Plicbox never sees those passwords; it keeps only the sign-in token, renewed automatically.

Changes go both ways: what you read, move, flag or delete in Plicbox changes on the server, and the other way round.

Without a Google account, calendars, contacts and tasks are kept on your PC: as many calendars as you like, with repeating events, plus contacts you add and everyone you write to, and task lists. They're in the daily backup, but not synced to other devices. Guests, video calls and Quick add need Google.

Mail, in detail

  • New mail: arrives as it lands when the server supports push (IMAP IDLE, which most do); otherwise Plicbox checks every two minutes. Every folder is refreshed every few minutes (5 by default, set in Settings).
  • Undo send: a message you send waits in the Outbox for 30 seconds first. Undo, next to Refresh, brings it back to edit. The wait can be 5 seconds to 5 minutes, or off (Settings → Mail → Sending).
  • Send later: the arrow next to Send picks a time (later today, tomorrow morning, Monday, or any date). The message waits in the Outbox until then. Plicbox sends it itself, so it needs to be running at that time (in the notification area is enough); otherwise it goes as soon as Plicbox starts.
  • Drag and drop while writing: pictures dropped on the text go into it; other files, and anything dropped elsewhere in the window, are attached.
  • Spelling: checked on your PC by Windows' own spell checker, in all the languages added to Windows at once, in one of them, or not at all (the button at the end of the formatting bar). Right-click an underlined word for suggestions.
  • Before you send: Plicbox points out attachments that may not get through: programs (even zipped), shortcuts, files with macros, password-locked zips, and more than 25 MB altogether.
  • Search: full text over subject, sender and body, on your PC (SQLite FTS5), so it's fast and private.
  • Trackers: pictures from the internet stay blocked until you choose to show them; known tracking pixels are removed even then. You can trust senders whose pictures should always load.
  • Phishing warnings: Plicbox reads the server's sender checks (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) and points out forged senders, replies that would go elsewhere, look-alike domains, links whose text names a different site, and attachments dressed up as documents (like invoice.pdf.exe).
  • Look-alike senders: a domain one letter off one you write to (or your own), or a known person's name on an address they've never used, gets a warning. The first message from an address is noted quietly.
  • Phishing and malware lists: senders and links on public lists of reported sites (OpenPhish, PhishTank, IPThreat and URLhaus, through malware-filter) are flagged in red. The lists download twice a day and are checked on your PC. Can be turned off (Settings → Mail → Phishing and malware).
  • Attachments you receive: zips are judged by what's inside, before you open them. What you open or save is marked as from the internet, so Office uses Protected View and your antivirus checks it first.
  • Rules and blocked senders: run on your PC as new mail arrives, so they work with any provider. They run while Plicbox is open, also from the notification area. A rule can move, copy, flag, archive or delete mail, mark it as read or move it to Junk, for instance when its sender or a link is on the phishing and malware lists.
  • Several messages at once: select them and right-click to block all their senders (or everyone at their companies' domains), or to create one rule for all of them.
  • Sender pictures: Gravatar, verified company logos (BIMI) and website icons, cached for a month. Optional.
  • Notifications: from the notification area, where Plicbox keeps running when you close the window (optional).

Offline

Plicbox works on your own copy, so a lost connection only means nothing new comes in. Everything you do in the meantime is kept and sent once you're back online:

  • Mail to read: new accounts download whole messages for the last year, attachments included, not just the list. Each account can keep the last month, 6 months, a year or everything, or download a message only when you open it (Accounts → Sync options → Offline mail). Settings → Mail shows how much space this takes.
  • Mail you send waits in the Outbox, under Favorites in the sidebar, and goes out on its own when the connection is back. If a server refuses a message, it stays there with the reason; open it to fix it.
  • Drafts are saved on your PC first and copied to the account's Drafts folder when it can be reached.
  • Read, flagged, moved or deleted: changed in Plicbox at once, and on the server later.
  • Google calendar and contacts: new events, changes, deletions, invitation answers and edited contacts show straight away and are sent to Google when you're back. If Google refuses one, Plicbox tells you why.

Your data

There is no Plicbox server. Mail, calendar, contacts and tasks are copied to your PC (%LOCALAPPDATA%\Plicbox) and synced directly with your provider. Passwords and sign-in tokens are kept in Windows Credential Manager or encrypted for your Windows account. There are no analytics and no tracking, and the source code is public (GPL, version 3), so anyone can check. The privacy policy lists every connection Plicbox makes.

Backups and files

  • Daily backup: once a day, Plicbox saves what exists only on your PC: calendars, contacts and tasks kept locally, your accounts and their settings, rules and signatures. Passwords aren't included (they stay in Windows Credential Manager), so on a new PC you sign in again. Mail isn't either: it stays on your mail servers and downloads again.
  • Backups go to a Plicbox Backups folder in OneDrive when you have it (so they're safe even if your PC isn't), otherwise in Documents. The newest 10 are kept. Change the folder, back up now or restore in Settings → Backup.
  • Calendars: export any calendar as an .ics file, or import one into it: right-click the calendar in the sidebar. An .ics file attached to an email has Add to Calendar.
  • Contacts: export to or import from a .vcf file (vCard 2.1, 3.0 and 4.0), from the … menu in Contacts.

Installing and updates

  • One installer, no administrator rights needed: Plicbox installs for your Windows user only.
  • The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn you once: choose More info → Run anyway.
  • Plicbox checks GitHub for new versions and downloads them in the background; they install when Plicbox restarts. You can turn this off in Settings and check by hand. Release notes.
  • To remove it: Windows Settings → Apps. To remove your data too, delete %LOCALAPPDATA%\Plicbox.

Language and look

  • English, German, Romanian and Spanish, switched in Settings.
  • Light or dark, following Windows or set by hand, with your own app font.
  • Your accent colour on buttons, and on links, selections and counts too (optional), in a shade that stays readable in light and dark.
  • In dark mode, plain messages are dark as well (optional). Those with their own colours or pictures, like newsletters, stay on a white page, and so does printing.
  • The message under the list, beside it, filling the screen, or only in its own window.
  • Font and size for mail that doesn't choose its own, and a zoom for every message.
  • A sidebar that folds into a strip of icons, and a unified inbox for all your accounts.

Not supported yet

  • Syncing calendars and contacts outside Google (CalDAV and CardDAV: Zoho, Fastmail, iCloud, Nextcloud and others). Planned; until then they can be kept on your PC, and moved in and out as files.
  • Exchange servers without IMAP: Plicbox speaks IMAP, not Exchange's own protocol.
  • POP3: IMAP only, so the same mail looks the same on every device.
  • macOS and Linux: Plicbox is a Windows app.
