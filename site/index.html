Questions

Good to know.

The details are in How Plicbox works.

Is it really free?

Yes: no subscription, no ads and no account to make. The source code is public on GitHub, under the GPL.

Where is my mail kept?

On your PC, in your Windows profile (%LOCALAPPDATA%\Plicbox). Passwords and sign-in tokens are kept by Windows, encrypted for your account. There is no Plicbox server, so nothing passes through us. More in the privacy policy.

Which accounts work?

Gmail and Google Workspace, with their calendars, contacts and tasks too. Outlook.com, Hotmail and Microsoft 365 for mail. And almost any provider with IMAP, such as iCloud, Yahoo, Fastmail, Zoho, GMX or your own domain. See every provider.

Does it work offline?

Partly. You can read, search and write without a connection, as new accounts keep the last year of mail on your PC, attachments included. New mail comes in, and what you send, move or delete goes out, once you're back online.

Why does Windows warn me when I install it?

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows may warn you once: choose More info > Run anyway. After that, Plicbox keeps itself up to date.

How do updates work?

Plicbox checks GitHub for new versions and installs them itself, fetching only what changed. You can turn automatic updates off in Settings.

Get Plicbox.

One small installer. No account to create, nothing to configure: sign in to your mail and you're done.
