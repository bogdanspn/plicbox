Privacy policy

Effective 10 November 2026, updated 13 November 2026

The short version: Plicbox is a desktop app that talks straight to your mail provider. There is no Plicbox server. Your mail, calendar, contacts, tasks, passwords and sign-in tokens stay on your PC and are never sent to the developer or anyone else. Plicbox has no analytics and no tracking.

Who we are

Plicbox is an email, calendar, contacts and tasks app for Windows, made by Cristian Bogdan Rosu, an independent developer (“we”, “us”). This policy covers the Plicbox app and this website, plicbox.bogdanrosu.net.

What Plicbox stores, and where

Everything Plicbox keeps is stored on your own PC, in your Windows user profile:

  • Your mail, calendar, contacts and tasks: a local copy, so you can read and search them offline. Stored in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Plicbox.
  • Passwords and sign-in tokens: kept in Windows Credential Manager, or encrypted for your Windows account, so other users of the PC can't read them.
  • Settings, cached sender pictures and log files: also in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Plicbox. The logs record what the app did, to help with troubleshooting, and are never uploaded. You only send them to us if you choose to.
  • Backups: once a day (you can turn this off), your accounts' settings, rules, signatures and the calendars, contacts and tasks kept on your PC are saved to a Plicbox Backups folder: in your OneDrive folder if you have one, otherwise in Documents, or wherever you choose in Settings → Backup. Mail, passwords and sign-in tokens are never included. A backup in OneDrive is uploaded by Microsoft's OneDrive app under your own account, not by Plicbox.

We have no copy of any of this and no way to see it.

Google account data

When you add a Gmail or Google Workspace account, you sign in on Google's own page in your browser. Plicbox then asks Google for the following access. Each one is used only for the feature described, between your PC and Google's servers directly:

AccessWhat Plicbox does with it
Gmail
https://mail.google.com/		 Downloads your mail so you can read and search it, sends the mail you write, and keeps read, flagged, moved and deleted messages in step with Gmail (IMAP and SMTP). Also reads your Gmail “Send mail as” addresses so you can send from them.
Google Calendar
…/auth/calendar		 Shows your calendars and events, and saves the events and invitation replies you create or change.
Google Contacts
…/auth/contacts		 Shows your contacts and saves the contacts you add or edit.
Other contacts
…/auth/contacts.other.readonly		 Suggests addresses of people you've written to as you type a recipient. Read-only.
Google Tasks
…/auth/tasks		 Shows your task lists and saves the tasks you add, complete or change.
Basic profile
openid email profile		 Identifies which account you signed in to and shows your name on it.

Limited Use

Plicbox's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide the features above, which you can see and use in the app.
  • It is never sent to the developer or to any third party, and never sold.
  • It is never used for advertising, and never used to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.
  • No person reads it. It stays on your PC.

Microsoft and other accounts

For Outlook.com and Microsoft 365 accounts, you sign in on Microsoft's page. Plicbox only asks for access to your mailbox over IMAP and to send mail over SMTP. For any other provider, Plicbox connects to that provider's mail servers with the details you enter. The same rules apply: the data stays on your PC.

Other connections the app makes

Apart from your mail providers, Plicbox only connects to the following. None of them receives the contents of your mail.

  • Updates: Plicbox checks GitHub for new versions and downloads them from our releases page. GitHub sees a normal web request from your PC. You can turn automatic updates off in Settings.
  • Sender pictures: to show a picture next to a sender, Plicbox may ask Gravatar for that sender's picture (Gravatar receives a one-way hash of the sender's address, not the address itself). It may also look up the sender's company logo published in DNS (BIMI), or a website icon for addresses like noreply@. Results are cached for about a month. You can turn this off in Settings (Show senders' pictures from the internet).
  • Setting up an account: to find the server settings for an address that isn't Google or Microsoft, Plicbox looks up the domain (the part after the @) in DNS and in Mozilla's public Thunderbird configuration database.
  • Pictures inside emails: pictures loaded from the internet are blocked until you choose to show them, and known tracking pixels are removed even then. When you show a message's pictures, the servers they come from see a normal request from your PC.

Your control

  • Remove an account in Plicbox to delete its local mail and its stored password or token.
  • Revoke access at any time at your Google Account permissions or your Microsoft account consents.
  • Delete everything: uninstall Plicbox and delete the folder %LOCALAPPDATA%\Plicbox.

Because we don't hold any of your data, there's nothing for us to export or delete on our side.

This website

This site is a few static pages with no cookies, analytics, trackers or third-party scripts. Like any web server, ours keeps standard access logs (such as IP address, page and time) for security and troubleshooting, kept for a short time and never shared. Download links take you to GitHub, where GitHub's privacy statement applies.

Children

Plicbox isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect anyone's personal information, of any age.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date. If Plicbox ever starts using your data in a new way, it will ask you first.
